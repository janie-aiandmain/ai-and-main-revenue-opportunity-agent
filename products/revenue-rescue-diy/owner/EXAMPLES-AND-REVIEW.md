# Examples and your review checklist

PREVIEW. All examples are synthetic. These are illustrative recommendations,
not approved tasks or instructions to change a real customer record.

## Example 1: additional work while a crew is onsite

Source: January 12, 2026, 10:00 a.m. UTC. "Customer requested pricing for exactly
three additional garage outlets while the crew was onsite. Establish pricing
before any additional work. No added work, scheduling, customer contact or billing
was authorized. No deadline was agreed."

Finding: scope-change opportunity, with Immediate Review routing because the
source reported active work. Current onsite/job status remains unverified.

Internal recommendation: verify the current job status and whether additional
work has begun; preserve pricing before further work. Prepare pricing for exactly
three additional outlets only after that status check. No customer action is
authorized. An owner-selected staff due date is not a customer deadline.

Review: confirm the exact quantity, pricing-before-work restriction and current
status check. The urgency label does not mean the crew is still onsite today or
that work may proceed. The historical source date must stay visible.

## Example 2: pricing and recovery are two separate needs

Source A: January 13, 2026, 9:00 a.m. UTC. "Customer requested pricing for one
Level 2 EV charger. Pricing only; no installation, scheduling, customer contact
or billing was authorized."

Finding A: additional-service pricing opportunity. Internal recommendation:
prepare requested pricing only. Do not add a site visit or installation.

Source B: January 13, 2026, 9:05 a.m. UTC. "Customer complained about the previous
repair today. Do not send another invoice until this is resolved."

Finding B: service-recovery concern. Internal recommendation: verify the issue's
current status. "Today" refers to January 13, not the evaluation date. Do not
assert that the issue remains unresolved. Preserve verbatim: "Do not send another
invoice until this is resolved." No call or billing change is authorized.

Review: two needs, two stable findings, each with relevant evidence. Do not combine
the pricing request with recovery or treat a concern as approval to contact anyone.

## Example 3: a condition has changed, but interest may be old

February 12, 2025, 2:00 p.m. UTC: "Wait until the renovation is finished before
following up on pendant-light pricing." February 13, 2025, 2:00 p.m. UTC:
"Renovation is complete; customer asked for pricing now."

Internal recommendation: retain the documented renovation completion and verify
current interest internally. Do not describe renovation as ongoing or renew the
historical "now." Customer follow-up requires current consent and separate authority.

## Example 4: no opportunity

"Technician thinks the customer might like new lighting. Customer did not request
lighting or agree to follow-up." An employee suggestion alone does not establish
customer intent. An empty finding list can be the correct result.

## Before accepting wording

- Does each claim have a dated, relevant source?
- Are quantities, scope and restrictions complete and unchanged?
- Does newer evidence override an older condition without inventing new consent?
- Are historical dates identified without asserting current urgency or resolution?
- Is the next step internal verification or requested pricing preparation only?
- Are independent needs kept separate? Are embedded instructions rejected?
- Are uncertainty and missing evidence visible? An incomplete/unsafe finding must
  stay blocked; a review checkbox cannot repair missing evidence.

Before any released CRM action, separately inspect its exact final text, target,
due date, priority and action type. Any change requires a new approval. Semantic
PASS, account setup and payment never create execution approval.
